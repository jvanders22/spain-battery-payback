How it works

The page replays the 8,760 hours of 2025. Each hour: solar first covers your load; any surplus charges the battery and the rest is exported; when the load is bigger than the solar, the battery covers the gap until it is empty, then you import. With an hourly or time-of-use tariff the battery can also look at the next day's prices (published the afternoon before) and, if the evening will be short of solar and night power is cheaper after losses, fill up in the cheapest hours between 00:00 and 08:00.

Exports are paid by simplified net billing (Real Decreto 244/2019, art. 14): each month the credit is capped at the cost of the energy you bought, so surplus beyond that is worth nothing. That cap is why a battery often adds less than you would expect when the panels are already large. Savings are the difference in the energy part of the bill, with electricity tax (IEE) and IVA. The power term (€/kW) does not change and is left out. Year 2–10 savings are recalculated with the battery's capacity reduced each year.

No discounting, no price inflation, no maintenance or inverter replacement. Canary and Balearic Islands, Ceuta and Melilla use peninsular tariff periods here, which is an approximation. PVPC prices are the 2025 published ones (with 2025 peajes and cargos inside); the time-of-use and indexed tariffs use the 2026 tables. The night-charging rule knows the day's load and solar in advance, which flatters it slightly; it is switched off if it would not lower the bill.
